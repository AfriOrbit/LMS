# The hardware-backed simulators

Four simulators in this build are driven by AfriOrbit's own hardware and CAD
rather than by illustrative numbers. This note says where each number comes
from, what is modelled, and what is not — so nobody has to guess.

## Where the data comes from

Two files under `src/content/` are **generated** and must not be hand-edited:

| File | Generated by | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `hardware.ts` (165 kB) | `scripts/extract-hardware.py` | six `.kicad_pcb` files in `EduSat-Project` and `SDR-IOT-project` |
| `geometry.ts` (649 kB) | `scripts/extract-geometry.py` | `EduSat v2/CAD/Cubesat cads.zip` |

Regenerate both with `npm run extract` (needs the repos cloned under `/tmp/ao`
and the CAD archive unzipped to `/tmp/cads`).

### The board files

`extract-hardware.py` is a real S-expression parser, not a regex sweep. It
pulls, per board: the Edge.Cuts outline (arcs flattened, segments stitched into
closed loops), the copper stackup, every footprint with its courtyard and
rotation, every pad resolved into board coordinates, every net, and the BOM.

The rotation maths was validated against KiCad's own pick-and-place export for
the IoT Edge Device: **68 of 88 placements match exactly**. Every one of the
remaining 20 is a known convention difference — the position file reports the
component *body centre* while `(at …)` is pin 1 — and each offset is an exact
half-pitch (6.25 mm on a 6-pin 2.5 mm JST, 3.75 mm on a 4-pin, 2.54 mm on a
TO-220). That is the strongest available evidence the parse is right.

Board mass is computed, not guessed: outline area by the shoelace formula on
the stitched loop, times the stackup thickness from the file's own
`(general (thickness …))`, times 1850 kg/m³ for FR-4, plus 34.3 g/m² per
copper layer.

### The CAD

`extract-geometry.py` decimates ~360 k triangles to ~33 k by vertex clustering
on a 2 mm grid, recomputes normals with a 50° hard-edge split, and ships the
result as base64 typed arrays (int16 positions in 0.01 mm counts, int8
normals). JSON number arrays would have tripled the size for no gain.

The body-frame transform is confirmed by a measurement it could not pass by
accident: the reframed bus envelope comes out **100.4 × 100.1 × 113.5 mm**,
and 113.5 mm is the CubeSat Design Specification rev 14 rail height exactly.
The generator asserts this and fails loudly if it drifts.

Two things worth knowing about the source archive: `S9`/`S10` and `S6`/`S7` are
byte-identical duplicate exports, so only one of each is loaded; and every
solid is wound inward, so every part's winding is flipped.

Part labels describe **where a part sits and what shape it is**, never what it
is for. The archive ships unnamed solids, and a viewer that confidently calls a
bracket a solar array teaches the wrong thing.

## The four simulators

- **`mission-designer`** — assemble a spacecraft from real modules, fly it for
  up to 14 days. Tracks battery state of charge against the eclipse cycle,
  accumulates payload data, and empties the buffer only during real ground
  station passes with a positive link margin. Reports mass against the CDS
  limit and names what failed.
- **`board-explorer`** — the six real PCBs, with net highlighting, part
  inspection and BOM lookup.
- **`orbit-lab`** — ground tracks, J2 drift, eclipse, pass prediction, Doppler
  and Walker constellations, from elements or a pasted TLE.
- **`spacecraft-viewer`** — the EduSat CAD in WebGL, lit by the real Sun vector
  and driven by the attitude law you pick.

## Persistence

There is none, deliberately. Every configuration lives in the query string as
base64url JSON holding **only the fields that differ from the defaults**, so a
lightly-edited design produces a short link and improving a default does not
invalidate old links. A version tag rides along; a mismatched version is
refused rather than misread. Decoding never throws — a mangled link falls back
to the defaults and says so.

## What the mission model does NOT do

Stated in the UI as well as here:

- **No thermal model.** Charging a li-ion pack below 0 °C damages it, and this
  simulation will not warn you.
- **No attitude dynamics.** Array incidence follows the pointing law you pick;
  it does not integrate torques. Tumbling is approximated by the convex-body
  average of ¼.
- **No radiation or single-event effects.** Ageing is a flat annual array
  degradation only.
- **Propagation is Kepler + J2 secular + the TLE `ndot` term, not SGP4.** A few
  kilometres of along-track error after a day in LEO. Fine for planning passes;
  not for conjunction analysis.

## Verification

`npm run check:physics` runs 231 assertions across four suites. The ones that
matter take their expected value from **outside this codebase**:

- GMST at J2000.0 against the IAU value, to 1e-5 degrees
- J2 nodal regression against an independent RK4 integration of the perturbed
  equations of motion (agrees to 0.4%, the residual being the mean-versus-
  osculating element distinction, which is explained in the test)
- the sun-synchronous inclination at 500 km (97.40°) and its 0.98565°/day drift
- the critical inclination, 63.4349°
- solstice and equinox sub-solar latitudes, perihelion and aphelion distances
- the Spacetrack Report #3 reference TLE, every field, checksums verified
- free-space path loss, the 1U mass limit, the 113.5 mm rail height
- the course's own forest-fire data-budget answers, reproduced exactly

`npm run verify` adds typecheck, lint, content checks and a production build.
A Playwright smoke test additionally confirms all four render in a real
browser, that the WebGL view actually rasterises, and that a shared link
restores its configuration.
